Conquering fixed-weight historical odd implements and official standardised certifications demands raw hand power, exceptional tendon density, and specialised finger and thumb clamping strength.

This page serves as the official Australian honour roll, chronicling the Australian athletes who have conquered the most prestigious benchmarks in grip history.

## The Thomas Inch Dumbbell

![Australian grip athlete deadlifting the 78kg Holle Thomas Inch Dumbbell replica in iconic Ugg boots](../../assets/images/australian-grip-feats-d11adc00.png)

The original **Thomas Inch Dumbbell** (famously known as the **"172"** or **"The Inch"**) was cast in 1906 by British physical culturist and professional strongman Thomas Inch (1881–1963). Cast as a single solid piece of iron, the dumbbell weighs **78.3 kg (172 lbs 9 oz)** with massive spherical globes and a thick, un-knurled handle measuring **60.3 mm (2 3/8 in)** in diameter and 10.1 cm (4 in) in length.

### Why is the Inch Dumbbell So Difficult?

1. **No Finger Overlap (Open-Hand Grip):** The 60.3 mm (2 3/8 in) handle prevents even large hands from wrapping the thumb over the fingers, eliminating any passive friction lock.
2. **Extreme Rotational Torque:** Because the heavy spherical bells are cast in one piece with the handle, the slightest tilt causes the implement to twist violently out of the lifter's fingers.
3. **Smooth Finish:** Traditional Inch dumbbells feature a cast-iron finish without knurling, testing pure friction and clamping force.

### Progression of Heavy Inch Bells

In addition to standard 78 kg replicas (such as those cast by Holle and Sorinex), heavy Inch replicas have been forged to push human limits:
* **78 kg (172.5 lb):** The standard Inch benchmark (Holle / Sorinex).
* **83 kg (183 lb):** Heritage Karbell (HK) replica.
* **95 kg (209 lb):** Strength of Scots (SoS) Wallace Challenge Circus Bell.
* **104 kg (229 lb):** Holle Mega-Inch Dumbbell.

## The York Blob

<div class="video-wrapper">
  <iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/Eqri9qfQvWA" title="What is the blob?" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture" allowfullscreen></iframe>
</div>

In 1992, legendary strength pioneer Richard Sorin sawed the head off a broken vintage York 45.4 kg (100 lb) round-headed dumbbell, christening the severed half **"The Blob"**. Weighed at approximately **22.7 kg (50 lb)**, it revolutionised block-weight training and became the international gold standard for wide pinch grip strength.

### The Challenge of the Blob

Unlike a flat pinch block, the Blob features steeply sloping, rounded, convex cast-iron faces. To lift it by the slick top and sloping sides requires immense open-hand thumb clamp pressure. There is no lip, ridge, or corner to catch — the weight must be hoisted purely by the frictional opposing pressure of the thumb against the opposing four fingers.

### Blob Variations Conquered in Australia

* **Original "Fatman" Blob:** Cut from early-generation York 45.4 kg (100 lb) round-head dumbbells, weighing 22.7 kg (50 lb); notorious for the widest flare and steepest taper.
* **Legacy Blob (York 120):** Cut from modern York Legacy 54.4 kg (120 lb) dumbbells (approx. 27.2 kg / 60 lb).
* **Holle Replicas:** Purpose-cast calibrated replicas produced at 24 kg (~53 lb) and 30 kg (~66 lb).
* **KWN 50:** Calibrated 22.7 kg (50 lb) steel block weight.
* **Blobzilla:** Cut from a 59 kg+ (130 lb+) York dumbbell (weighing upwards of 29.5 kg / 65 lb).

## Captains of Crush Grippers

Since their introduction in 1988 by IronMind, **Captains of Crush (CoC)** torsion-spring grippers have served as the undisputed benchmark of human crushing grip strength. While thousands of gym-goers train with entry-level grippers, closing the legendary **Captains of Crush No. 3** (127 kg / ~280 lb of calibrated pressure) under strict official certification rules is one of the rarest feats in all of strength sports.

### Certification Rules

To earn official certification from IronMind:
1. The attempt must be officiated by an official IronMind referee or verified through unedited continuous high-definition video.
2. The handles must be set with an official **IronMind Credit Card** (54 mm width) placed between the handle ends immediately before the final crush.
3. The handles must make complete, audible contact and remain held closed under full control.

## Crushed-to-Dust! Challenge

Introduced by IronMind in 2012, the **Crushed-to-Dust! Challenge** tests a lifter's all-around grip competence across three fundamentally different mechanical stress vectors. All three feats must be successfully executed within a strict **3-minute time limit**:

1. **Crushing Grip:** Close a Captains of Crush No. 2 gripper (88.5 kg / ~195 lb).
2. **Revolving Thick Bar:** Deadlift **90 kg (198 lb)** on the 50.8 mm (2 in) diameter IronMind Rolling Thunder handle.
3. **Key / Hub Pinch:** Deadlift **20 kg (44 lb)** on the IronMind Hub by pinching the smooth, circular key boss.

## Submitting a Feat

Have you completed an iconic grip feat or certified on an official implement? Grip Australia maintains this register as a permanent record of Australian strength history.

### Verification Guidelines for Submission

* **Continuous, Unedited Video:** Video must capture the attempt from initial setup to completion without cuts or camera shifts.
* **Implement Inspection:** Show the implement clearly, including manufacturer markings, weight stamps, or scale verification where relevant.
* **Proper Lockout:** Clear full-body lockout with knees locked, hips extended, and shoulders back.
* **Controlled Descent:** The implement must be returned to the floor under control (no dropping).
