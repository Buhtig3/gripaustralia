## Equipment Calibration: How RGC Works vs Factory Numbers

Torsion-spring hand grippers are a widely adopted benchmark of crushing power, but stamped factory numbers create immense confusion. Here is the engineering science behind why manufacturer ratings mislead, how the RGC system standardizes closing force, and why calibration is essential.

### 1. The Myth of "Pounds to Close"

Commercial grippers are frequently marketed with round numbers—such as "100 lb", "200 lb", or "300 lb" to close. These numbers represent **idealized mathematical mid-spring ratings**, not the actual force required by human fingers at the handle tips.

- **Manufacturer Ratings (Mid-Spring Force):** Measured or modeled directly at the spring coil or halfway up the handle. This ignores the mechanical lever advantage created by the athlete's hand applying pressure further down the handle.
- **RGC True Force (End-of-Handle Force):** Because hand grippers function as Class 3 levers, applying pressure at the base of the handle requires approximately **40% to 50% less raw force** than the stamped factory number suggests. A certified Captains of Crush #3 rated nominally at 280 lbs actually requires approximately **140 to 150 lbs of true closing force** when measured at the tip.

---

### 2. The RGC Testing Rig Standard

**RGC (Redneck Gripper Calibration)**, pioneered by Cannon PowerWorks and adopted globally, removes all human subjectivity:

1. **Rigid Horizontal Mounting:** The gripper is clamped horizontally by one handle into a rigid steel fixture.
2. **Standardised Calibration Point:** A 1/4" non-elastic strap is positioned precisely at the very tip of the free handle.
3. **Free Weight Loading:** Calibrated weight plates are added vertically below the strap until the handle ends make continuous parallel contact.
4. **Permanent Stamped Tagging:** The final suspended weight (including loading pin and attachment hardware) is recorded to 0.1 lb precision and stamped onto an aluminum tag affixed to the gripper coil.

---

### 3. Factory Variance & Why Progression Fails

Two identical grippers from the same manufacturer can differ substantially due to manufacturing tolerances:

- **Spring Wire Thickness:** Minor variances in wire gauge (even &plusmn;0.05 mm) cause noticeable shifts in torsional stiffness.
- **Mounting Depth:** A spring pressed 2 mm deeper into an aluminum handle significantly increases the required closing torque.
- **Handle Spread:** Wide handle spreads increase the initial travel distance, demanding greater finger extension strength.

Without calibrated RGC numbers, an athlete jumping from what they believe is a 130 lb gripper to a 160 lb gripper may actually be attempting a jump from 120 lbs to 175 lbs. Calibration eliminates guesswork from programming.
